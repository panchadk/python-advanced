Vectorization — no loops needed

An operation on an array applies to every element at once. This is shorter than a loop and dramatically faster, because the work happens in optimized C under the hood.

In [ ]: python3
TIP Rule of thumb: if you find yourself writing a for loop over a NumPy array, there's almost always a vectorized version that's faster and clearer.

Lesson

Indexing, slicing & boolean masks

Arrays index like lists, but add two superpowers: multi-dimensional slicing, and boolean masks that select elements by condition.

In [ ]: python3

Lesson

2-D arrays and axes

Real data is usually 2-D: rows and columns. The axis argument chooses the direction an operation runs — axis=0 down columns, axis=1 across rows.

In [ ]: python3

Lesson

Broadcasting

Broadcasting lets NumPy combine arrays of different shapes by automatically stretching the smaller one. It's how you add a vector to every row of a matrix without a loop.

In [ ]: python3
WHY IT MATTERS Broadcasting rules: NumPy compares shapes from the right; dimensions must be equal or one of them must be 1. Master this and most 'reshape' headaches disappear.

Lesson

A little linear algebra

NumPy's @ operator does matrix multiplication, and np.linalg handles inverses, determinants, and solving systems — the machinery behind regression and many ML models.
