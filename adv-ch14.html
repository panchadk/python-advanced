Unsupervised learning

So far every model learned from labelled answers. Unsupervised learning finds structure in data with no labels at all — grouping similar items (clustering) and compressing many features into a few (dimensionality reduction). The first cell installs scikit-learn; every cell runs live.

K-means clustering

K-means groups data into k clusters by repeatedly assigning points to the nearest center and moving the centers to the mean of their points. You choose k; the algorithm finds the groupings.

In [ ]: python3
TIP K-means needs you to pick k in advance, and it assumes roughly round, similar-sized clusters. When you don't know k, the 'elbow method' (plotting inertia vs k) helps you choose.

Lesson

Evaluating clusters without labels

With no ground truth, you judge clusters by their shape. The silhouette score (−1 to 1) measures how tight and well-separated the clusters are — higher is better.

In [ ]: python3

Lesson

PCA: dimensionality reduction

Principal Component Analysis compresses many correlated features into a few 'principal components' that capture most of the variation. Great for visualization and for speeding up models.

In [ ]: python3
WHY IT MATTERS Those two components capture the vast majority of the variance in four original features — which is why you can often plot high-dimensional data in 2-D and still see its structure.

Lesson

Putting it together

A common pipeline: reduce dimensions with PCA, then cluster in the smaller space. Faster, and often cleaner clusters because noise is stripped away.

In [ ]: python3

Lesson

When to use unsupervised learning

Reach for it when you have no labels but want to understand your data: customer segments, topic groups, anomaly detection, or just a 2-D map of something high-dimensional. It's exploration and compression, not prediction.

EX 1

Cluster into 3 groups

Run K-means with 3 clusters (random_state=0, n_init=10) on the iris data and print the three cluster sizes as a list.

Show solution

from sklearn.datasets import load_iris from sklearn.cluster import KMeans import numpy as np X, y = load_iris(return_X_y=True) labels = KMeans(n_clusters=3, random_state=0, n_init=10).fit_predict(X) print(np.bincount(labels).tolist())

EX 2

Variance captured by PCA

Reduce the iris data to 2 components and print the total fraction of variance explained, rounded to 3 decimals.

In [ ]: python3
Show solution

from sklearn.datasets import load_iris from sklearn.decomposition import PCA X, y = load_iris(return_X_y=True) p = PCA(n_components=2).fit(X) print(round(p.explained_variance_ratio_.sum(), 3))
