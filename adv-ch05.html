Exploratory data analysis (EDA)

Before modeling, you explore. EDA is the disciplined first look at a dataset — its shape, its distributions, its relationships, and its surprises. This chapter walks the EDA workflow on a real-feeling table. The first cell installs pandas; every cell runs live.

Lesson

Setup & first look

The first move with any dataset: look at its shape, a few rows, and its columns. Run this once.

Lesson

Summary statistics

describe() gives count, mean, std, min, quartiles, and max for every numeric column at once — your fastest read on central tendency and spread.

In [ ]: python3

Lesson

Distributions

Look at how a single variable is spread: its mean vs median (a gap hints at skew), and its range. Understanding each variable alone comes before looking at relationships.

In [ ]: python3
TIP When the mean is much higher than the median, the data is right-skewed (a few large values pull the mean up). Income and prices are classic examples — worth knowing before you model them.

Lesson

Relationships: correlation

Correlation measures how strongly two numeric variables move together, from -1 to +1. corr() gives you the whole matrix at once — a fast way to spot what relates to what.

In [ ]: python3

Lesson

Group comparisons

EDA often means comparing groups. Combine groupby with summaries to see how a metric differs across categories — often the most useful insight in the whole analysis.

In [ ]: python3

Lesson

The EDA mindset

EDA isn't a fixed checklist — it's a habit of curiosity. Look, ask 'does that make sense?', and follow surprises. The goal is to understand the data well enough to model it wisely (and to catch problems before they poison a model).
